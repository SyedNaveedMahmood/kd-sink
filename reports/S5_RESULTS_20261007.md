## Findings in brief

At the Full300 step-10,000 endpoint, C2 (full-attention supervision) and C6 (binary sink-versus-rest supervision) had high first-position sink mass, `S = 0.332` and `0.357`; C1 (logit KD base) and C5 (conditional non-sink supervision) remained low, `S = 0.017` and `0.024`. Full attention teacher–student JSD was lowest for C2 (`0.0504`), then C6 (`0.0668`), C5 (`0.1877`), and C1 (`0.2288`). Conditional non-sink JSD told a different story: C5 was lowest (`0.0638`), C2 next (`0.0753`), C1 (`0.1000`), and C6 highest (`0.1072`). These are distinct descriptive attention metrics; subtracting one from the other would **not** yield a valid sink contribution.

Clean CE differed much less than attention similarity. At step 10,000, C1/C2/C5/C6 clean CE was `3.9945/3.9934/3.9783/3.9991` nats per target. C5 had the smallest recorded CE but the report makes no equivalence, noninferiority, or population utility claim. The same endpoint's all-layer first-key deletion increased CE by `0.0512/0.3734/0.0700/0.2273` nats per target for C1/C2/C5/C6; relocation showed `0.0795/0.3663/0.1010/0.3481`. Thus full attention matching, conditional non-sink matching, clean prediction, sink mass, and intervention sensitivity do not rank the four training recipes identically.

The time course also separates pattern from measured dependence. By Dense64 step 100, C2 and C6 already had `S = 0.457` and `0.392`, but deletion `ΔCE` was `0.0329` for C2 and slightly negative (`−0.0030`) for C6. Their deletion costs grew later. No approved threshold defines a first functional step, and 101 evaluation points are repeated measurements of one trained seed per condition, not independent replicates.

## Design, measurements, and interpretation

S5 asks how supervising full attention (C2), conditional attention after excluding key 0 (C5), or binary sink mass versus rest (C6) compares with the same CE/logit-KD behavioral base alone (C1). The registered base is `0.5 CE + 0.5 logit KD` with KD temperature 2; C2/C5/C6 add their distinct attention targets with coefficient `1/9` and scale 1. C5 removes key 0 **before** alignment/divergence, so its auxiliary loss has no direct sink-score target, though shared parameters, CE, and KD may still alter sinks. C6 supervises the two-way sink-versus-rest probability and does not directly supervise the detailed distribution among non-sink keys. The auxiliary targets differ in information content; raw training objective values are not a common scale of scientific fidelity. See the [S5 study](../design/e6a_v2/studies/S5_SINK_SUPERVISION.md) and [objective contract](../design/e6a_v2/OBJECTIVES.md).

The source experiment used a frozen GPT-2-large teacher (36 layers, 20 heads, width 1,280) and GPT-2-medium-architecture students (24 layers, 16 heads, width 1,024) initialized randomly from configuration, not pretrained student weights. The common frozen schedule used microbatch 4 × accumulation 16 = 64 sequences of 128 input tokens per optimizer update, or 8,192 input tokens and 8,128 shifted targets per update. The 10,000-update source runs therefore record 81,920,000 input tokens and 81,280,000 targets. AdamW used peak LR `5e-4`, 500-update warmup, cosine decay to the 10% floor, betas `(0.9, 0.95)`, epsilon `1e-8`, weight decay `0.1`, and gradient clipping at norm 1. Original S1 compute used BF16 autocast with FP32 parameters/optimizer states and probability/loss reductions. D26 binds these shared architecture, numerical, and schedule fields; S5 did not retune them.

S5 reused previously recorded S1 student evaluations on the same frozen OpenWebText panels. `owt_dense64` has 64 blocks and 8,128 valid shifted targets at steps 0, 100, …, 10,000 (101 points). `owt_full300` has 300 blocks and 38,100 valid targets at steps 0, 100, 250, 500, 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, 7,500, and 10,000. All four conditions use the same panel members and order within each panel. The panel-manifest SHA is `03fa2adf3931f7594fae23d70e402c697ad2c771e53bdb68977b614336cc5013`; Dense64 item-ID SHA is `19fe2226e768410baa1d2addf891b71e0a2e482a22b2b9459905b8bd13b2bb83`, Full300 item-ID SHA `5298fa0d63e7743077e60e6ab0afe254fb077891ab4d06ae648bc74cda8f7fce`. S1 evaluation used its original BF16 numerical policy and each source run's fixed hardware identity. S5 did not recalculate model outputs at FP32 or any other precision.

`S` is the native-layer mean later-query attention mass assigned to key position 0. `Full JSD` and `non-sink JSD` are common *descriptive* teacher–student head-mean attention comparisons on the registered mapped layers and masks; they are not interchangeable with the differently constructed training losses. `ΔCE = CE_edited − CE_clean` in nats per valid next-token target; positive means the intervention worsened target likelihood. Self-KL compares the clean output distribution to the edited distribution; flips are the fraction of top-1 next-token predictions that changed. Absolute target-logprobability change retains magnitude when signed `ΔCE` cancels across targets. Deletion and relocation edit attention before value aggregation over **all 24 native student layers**; the mapped teacher comparison uses 24 of 36 teacher layers. These are first-key interventions, not selective removal of all possible sink circuits. The [metric contract](../design/e6a_v2/METRICS_AND_ANALYSIS.md) fixes the reductions and cautions against equating output sensitivity with usefulness for clean prediction.
